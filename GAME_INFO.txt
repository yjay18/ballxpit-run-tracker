PIT CREW — BALL x PIT GAME AND TRACKER GUIDE
============================================

Purpose
-------
This document explains the systems represented by Pit Crew and gives a quick
reference for playing BALL x PIT alongside the tracker. It is fan-maintained,
not official documentation. Numbers and recipes can change between game
updates; the in-game encyclopedia should win if it disagrees with this file.


1. THE BASIC LOOP
-----------------
BALL x PIT mixes a physics-based combat run with persistent town building.
During a run, the player fires balls into ranks of enemies. Balls bounce from
walls, strike enemies, apply statuses, spawn extra projectiles, or create area
effects. Defeated enemies drop experience and resources. Level-up choices add
or improve balls and passive items. Mini-boss rewards and Fusion Reactors let
the player combine a mature build into stronger forms.

Between runs, resources are spent in the town. Buildings unlock characters,
raise account-wide attributes, expand utility, and affect future runs. A run
build therefore begins with three layers of state:

1. permanent town upgrades;
2. the selected character and that character's special rule;
3. balls and passives collected during the current run.

Pit Crew currently focuses on layers 2 and 3. Town values are useful context
for judgment, but are not yet editable in the interface.


2. TOWN ECONOMY AND HARVESTING
------------------------------
The primary construction resources are Wheat, Wood, and Stone. Gold pays for
grid expansions, market exchanges, and some utility actions. Resource nodes
regenerate over real time up to their capacity.

Harvesting is an active physics phase. Workers are launched through the town
and gather from buildings they touch. Dense arrangements, corridors, and
U-shaped pockets create additional rebounds before the timer expires. The
Watch Tower can extend Harvest time. Farms, Lumberyards, and Stone Mines
process adjacent resource nodes on their own schedules.

Permanent stat buildings generally fall into two groups:

- Flat attributes: Consulate, Schoolhouse, Shoemaker, Gunsmith, Barracks, and
  Clinic.
- Percentage scaling: Diplomacy Hall, University, Wheelwright, Archery Range,
  Military Academy, and Alchemist.

Biome trophies scale with the number of distinct characters used to clear a
biome and can receive further value from fast clears. Utility buildings such
as the Adventurer's Guild, Matchmaker, and Necromancer alter the rules of a
run by enabling Endless Mode, dual characters, or a one-time revive.


3. THE SIX CHARACTER ATTRIBUTES
-------------------------------
Endurance: raises maximum health and the value of close-range play.

Strength: raises direct impact damage before critical and item multipliers.

Leadership: increases baby-ball capacity and damage. It is the core stat for
Brood Mother, Egg Sac, Cell, and other swarm engines.

Speed: affects character movement and projectile velocity. Faster projectiles
can improve coverage, while slower projectiles sometimes gain value from
bounce-scaling effects.

Dexterity: improves firing frequency and critical hit output. It is valuable
for effects that need many contacts or stacks.

Intelligence: improves area size, status damage and duration, and passive item
potency. It is central to AOE and damage-over-time builds.

Attributes do not have equal value for every character. The Tactician makes
Speed irrelevant because combat is turn-based. The Makeshift Sisyphus removes
direct impact damage and multiplies AOE/status damage. The Tiptoer is so fragile
that shields and avoidance matter more than simply stacking Endurance.


4. CHARACTER BUILD IDENTITIES
------------------------------
The Warrior — starts with Bleed. Neutral rules make this the baseline character.

The Itchy Finger — starts with Burn. Fires rapidly and continuously with
scattered aim; favor on-hit statuses and nearby AOE coverage.

The Repentant — starts with Freeze. Gains stacking damage per bounce and hurts
enemies on the return trip; favor rebound and back-wall tools.

The Cohabitants — start with Brood Mother. Mirrored half-damage shots double
contacts; favor status application and baby-ball engines.

The Cogitator — starts with Laser (Vertical). Chooses upgrades automatically;
use the tracker mainly to record and interpret the resulting build.

The Embedded — starts with Poison. Native pierce spreads effects through enemy
columns; area and status evolutions are high-value.

The Shade — starts with Dark. Fires from the far side and has base critical
chance; backstab and directional-critical passives are natural fits.

The Shieldbearer — starts with Iron. A frontal shield reflects balls; slow,
heavy shots and repeated-bounce damage are especially useful.

The Spendthrift — starts with Vampire. Fires the full inventory in one wide
volley; keep levels even and protect the health economy.

The Juggler — starts with Lightning. Lobs balls to a chosen landing point;
precision AOE matters more than conventional straight-line pierce.

The Empty Nester — starts with Ghost. Fires multiple special balls but no
normal baby balls; avoid passives that only improve baby balls.

The Flagellant — starts with Egg Sac. Balls rebound from a lower barrier rather
than simply returning; persistent swarm and bounce effects stay active longer.

The Makeshift Sisyphus — starts with Earthquake. Direct contact deals no damage,
while AOE and statuses are multiplied four times. Pure impact recommendations
must be rejected unless they are ingredients for a useful AOE evolution.

The Physicist — starts with Light. Gravity pulls balls toward the back of the
screen; back-wall triggers and row-wide effects benefit.

The Tactician — starts with Iron. Combat is turn-based, so Speed and velocity
passives have no meaningful value.

The Radical — starts with Wind. Aims, moves, and chooses upgrades automatically;
the tracker acts as a spectator and route analyzer.

The Falconer — starts with Lightning. Two side falcons launch the balls; favor
ranged coverage and avoid pairing with layouts that create unreachable zones.

The Carouser — starts with Charm. Returning balls can orbit the player; close-
range damage and defenses complement this kinetic shield.

The Tunneller — starts with Earthquake. Balls wrap vertically; persistent AOE
and statuses can make repeated passes through the formation.

The Tiptoer — starts with Laser (Horizontal). Most normal enemies cannot detect
the character, but very few hits are survivable; shields and control come first.

The False Messiah — starts with Bleed. Twitch viewers can influence choices and
events; resilient, flexible recipes tolerate that uncertainty.

The Hoary Hoarder — has no starting ball, only 2 ball slots, and up to 8 passive
slots. Choose one realistic ball recipe and focus on passive artifacts.

The Ballbearer — starts with Stone, can carry up to 10 balls, and cannot equip
passives. This is the best environment for nested, multi-tier recipes.


5. BALL LEVELS, FISSION, FUSION, AND EVOLUTION
----------------------------------------------
Base and evolved balls can reach level 3.

Fission distributes one or more random level gains among equipped items. It is
usually the safe early choice because it raises current power without reducing
projectile count. If everything is already maxed, Fission can become Gold.

Fusion combines two level-3 balls to free a slot and retain both properties.
The immediate projectile count drops, so a poorly timed Fusion can reduce wave
clear until the open slot is filled again.

Evolution is a specific recipe. Every ball component must be level 3. The
ingredients are consumed and replaced by the evolved ball. Pit Crew's compact
recommendation panel detects ready recipes and performs the same inventory
transformation in the tracker.

Passive artifacts are different: their components only need to be held, not
leveled. Combining them frees a passive slot. Pit Crew still records levels
1-3 for both ordinary passives and evolved artifacts so the displayed loadout
matches the current in-game run.

Slot-lock happens when every slot contains a different unfinished recipe piece.
There is then no room to take the missing component and no valid merge. Pick a
target, level compatible pairs, and avoid opening too many branches at once.


6. BASE BALL IDENTITIES
-----------------------
Bleed — stacking wound damage triggered by later projectile contacts.
Brood Mother — chance to create a baby ball on enemy impact.
Burn — short-duration true damage over time.
Cell — splits into copies after impact.
Charm — may turn an enemy against its allies.
Dark — very high impact multiplier, then destroys itself and waits to refire.
Earthquake — area damage around the impact tile.
Egg Sac — bursts into several baby balls and has a refire delay.
Freeze — immobilizes and amplifies damage received.
Ghost — passes through enemies.
Iron — heavy double-damage projectile with reduced movement speed.
Laser (Horizontal) — damages the full row.
Laser (Vertical) — damages the full column.
Light — blinds targets and causes melee misses.
Lightning — arcs damage into nearby enemies.
Poison — longer stacking damage over time.
Stone — begins with very high damage that erodes through successive targets.
Time — creates a time snare and feeds chronological evolutions.
Vampire — has a chance to heal on enemy contact.
Wind — pierces and slows at the cost of direct damage.

8. PASSIVES AND ARTIFACTS
-------------------------
Passives alter projectile physics, damage, defenses, summons, economy, and the
health loop. Some are conditional and should not be expected in the loot pool
until a compatible effect is already held. Examples:

- Midnight Oil needs a Burn-applying ball.
- Frozen Spike needs Freeze.
- Voodoo Doll needs Curse.
- Silver Blindfold needs Blind.
- Bandage Roll is useful only when healing occurs often enough to trigger it.

Important mechanical loops:

- Vampiric Sword + Everflowing Goblet becomes Soul Reaver. Add Bandage Roll so
  repeated healing also launches free baby balls.
- Ethereal Cloak loses its pierce after the back-wall impact. Ghostly Corset
  creates Phantom Regalia and preserves the intended pierce cycle. Upturned
  Hatchet is a fallback that makes the rebound valuable.
- Brood Mother or Egg Sac makes War Horn and Baby Rattle stronger. Together the
  passives become Cornucopia and further multiply spawned balls.
- Wretched Onion and Breastplate become Odiferous Shell, turning dangerous
  close contact into a chance to execute enemies.

9. HOW PIT CREW RECOMMENDS ITEMS
--------------------------------
The recommendation engine is heuristic. It starts with the selected character's
curated priorities and exclusions. It then adjusts scores using current state:

- A ball receives a large bonus when it completes an evolution with a held ball.
- A passive receives a large bonus when it completes a held artifact recipe.
- Baby-ball passives rise when the loadout can already spawn baby balls.
- Magic Staff rises when the loadout already deals AOE damage.
- Conditional passives remain hidden until their required status is present.

Absolute character rules are applied before general synergy. Direct-damage-only
ideas are penalized for Sisyphus. Speed passives are removed for Tactician.
Baby-ball-only tools are removed for Empty Nester. Passive suggestions disappear
entirely for Ballbearer.

The result is a live decision aid, not a mathematical proof. Enemy composition,
town upgrades, player execution, unlocked loot, and game patches can all change
the best practical choice.


10. USING THE TRACKER DURING A RUN
----------------------------------
1. Press Start a Run and choose the current character.
2. Add every ball and passive when it is taken in-game.
3. Use the minus and plus controls to match ball and passive levels. This also
   applies to evolved balls and evolved passive artifacts.
4. Standard characters display 4 ball slots and 4 passive slots. Character-
   specific limits still override this where needed.
5. Read the compact Recommended Next panel for the strongest two available
   balls and passives.
6. Use Compare Offer when the game presents 2-3 choices. Select only those
   choices, rank them, and tap Choose to add the selected pickup immediately.
7. Pin an evolution or artifact target to prioritize its missing ingredients.
8. Compact mode reduces selected items to icon-focused controls. Undo restores
   the most recent add, removal, level change, evolution, or target change.
9. When a recipe is ready, Evolve or Forge appears in the same panel. Use it
   only after performing the corresponding action in-game.
10. The state saves automatically in the current browser. End Run clears it.
